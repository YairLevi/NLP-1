what the top-10 5-grams show

english 5-grams are generic function-word phrases: "at the end of the", "in the United States on", "for the first time in", "as a result of the", "at the time of the". they are the n-gram version of zipf's law: only closed-class words and very common collocations repeat this often. the case-sensitive count also splits "at the end of the" (3,614) from "At the end of the" (1,695), because tokens keep their case.

hebrew 5-grams are more topical and more rigid: "עם פרוץ מלחמת העולם השנייה" / "הראשונה", "המשפט העליון של ארצות הברית", "שנות ה-90 של המאה ה-20", "העשור הראשון של המאה ה-21". these are fixed encyclopedic phrases. a few come from templated sentences on sports-biography pages ("ערך את הופעת הבכורה שלו", "במשחה ל-200 מטר מעורב אישי"). the top hebrew counts (734) are about five times lower than the english one (3,614): the file is smaller, and prefixes such as ה-, ב-, ל-, ש- are glued to words, so the same phrase is spelled with many different tokens and its count is split.

what the top-10 10-grams show

at length 10 almost nothing is free language any more. the lists are dominated by boilerplate repeated across many articles: tv-episode templates ("aired on the Fox network in the United States on", 316), ship infoboxes ("at a speed of 10 knots 19 km h 12 mph"), a book title in a citation (the Simpsons guide, 206), and in hebrew military-career sentences ("עבר מסלול הכשרה כלוחם קורס מ"כים חי"ר וקורס קציני חי"ר", 69), a hebrew calendar template ("על פי הלוח העברי הקבוע פרשת בר המצווה של ילד שנולד") and wikipedia chart code ("PlotArea left:70 right:40 ..."). the lists contain many entries that are the same phrase shifted by one token, so their counts are almost equal (206, 206, 206 in english). a phrase of length 11 contributes two 10-grams, and so on. a count near that of a longer phrase means that the 10-gram is just a window of it.

longest n-gram with frequency above 1

english: n = 16,559 tokens, frequency 2 (a single such n-gram). it is the whole article "Chevrolet Volt", which appears twice in the file. hebrew: n = 2,185 tokens, frequency 2, a long passage about jerusalem (the declaration of jerusalem as capital, 1949) that occurs twice in the file. in both files the longest repeat is therefore a copy-paste duplicate and not language structure; frequency 2 means exactly one copy. every sub-n-gram of it has frequency at least 2, which is why the number of repeated n-grams stays large (hundreds of thousands) for very large n.

how repeated n-grams decay with n

distinct repeated n-grams (frequency > 1) first grow (several million 2- to 4-grams) and then fall; the number of positions covered by a repeated n-gram falls too (table above). at n = 5, 15.5% of english token positions start a repeated 5-gram, but only 6.4% for hebrew. the fall is slower than a random process would give: from n = 32 on, the surviving positions are mostly duplicated passages, so the curve becomes nearly flat. english has more repeated material than hebrew (longer repeat, larger share), consistent with its larger file and its less rich morphology.

method (short)

an n-gram can occur more than once only if its (n-1)-prefix and (n-1)-suffix do (hint of the assignment). the script keeps only start positions of frequent (n-1)-grams, extends them by one token, counts the extensions with one numpy unique over int64 keys and keeps those with count above 1. tokens are int32 ids and the token array is cached on disk. for n above 32 the survivors are tested with a rolling hash and a binary search on n, and the final answer is verified token by token.
